Session 7: Discrete & Continuous Distributions

Introduction to Statistical Methods — Master the core probability models visually.

1. Overview

Uncertainty is at the heart of statistics. Probability distributions give us a full map of how likely different outcomes are. We split them into discrete distributions (counting distinct outcomes) and continuous distributions (measuring smooth quantities).

The Core Intuition

Think of a probability distribution as a recipe. It tells you the chance of every possible event before you collect any data.

2. Bernoulli Distribution

A Bernoulli trial is a single experiment with exactly two outcomes: Success (\(X = 1\)) with probability \(p\), or Failure (\(X = 0\)) with probability \(q = 1 - p\).

3. Binomial Distribution

When you perform \(n\) independent Bernoulli trials under equal conditions, the total count of successes follows a Binomial distribution.

\[ P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k} \]

4. Poisson Distribution

The Poisson distribution counts rare events happening over a fixed interval of time or space, given a constant average rate \(\lambda\).

\[ P(X = k) = \frac{e^{-\lambda} \lambda^k}{k!} \]

5. Normal Distribution

The Normal distribution is the classic bell curve. Continuous measurements like weights, scores, and heights naturally follow this curve.
